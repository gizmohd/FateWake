# Season One — 90-Day World Bible

## Purpose

Season One is structured as nine ten-day acts. This document defines the narrative and systems backbone, not ninety rigid scripts.

Each act advances four things together:

1. the player's personal survival story,
2. the social scale of the world,
3. the mystery of The Silence,
4. Wakes created earlier in the season.

The season begins as an intimate survival story and ends with the realization that isolated survivor histories are becoming a shared world.

> **Every choice leaves a wake.**

## Structural Rules

- Every new player begins with Act I and experiences The Silence personally.
- Survivor Day and broader world/timeline state are distinct.
- Early play is primarily NPC-driven.
- Real-player contact is introduced as a meaningful story event, not ambient MMO population.
- Timelines intersect gradually and only share facts appropriate to their scope.
- Personal decisions remain true after convergence even when regional or global outcomes differ.
- Major consequences should often be delayed.
- Failure changes the story instead of routinely ending it.
- AI narrates deterministic outcomes; it does not determine canonical truth.
- The exact cause of The Silence remains unresolved during this 90-day arc.

# Act I — The Silence
## Days 1–10
### Question: What happened?
### Evolution: Individual → Survivor

Normal life ends. Power, communications and familiar assumptions fail. The player learns that people are more dangerous, valuable and complicated than simple resource counters.

### Opening State
The player begins at 6:17 AM in an ordinary place. Maya, Eli and the injured stranger create the first immediate human problem. The first anomalous broadcast establishes that something is wrong beyond an ordinary blackout.

### Central Conflict
Stay isolated and preserve resources, or begin accepting responsibility for other people.

### Mystery Progression
- Future-dated 6:17 transmission can be discovered.
- Powered devices fail in a synchronized event.
- Broadcasts function when they should not.
- Warning references familiar voices.
- Noah's missing parents introduce unexplained disappearances.
- Day 7 asks humanity to answer while another voice says not to.

### Systems Introduced
Personal resources, relationships, NPC interaction, free-form intent, Wakes, journal, behavioral identity, delayed consequences and the first global-choice concept.

### Wake Seeds
- Maya first impression.
- Fate of the injured stranger.
- Treatment of Noah.
- Pharmacy/insulin resolution.
- Discovery or ignorance of the future-dated broadcast.
- Day 7 signal response.

### Climax
The Day 7 signal asks humanity to answer. Days 8–10 show subtle consequences without explaining what answering actually did.

### Hook
Evidence appears that the player is not merely surviving a communications outage. Someone—or something—is observing patterns of response.

---

# Act II — The Others
## Days 11–20
### Question: Who else survived?
### Evolution: Survivor → Group Member

The player's immediate neighborhood becomes a social organism. Survival now requires rules, trust, labor and uncomfortable compromises.

### Central Conflict
Who belongs, who contributes, and who gets protected when resources cannot cover everyone?

### Mystery Progression
- Distant radio fragments contain identifiers that do not match known emergency systems.
- Multiple survivors report hearing familiar voices.
- Accounts of the first morning conflict in small but unsettling ways.
- A repeating sequence appears inside 6:17 transmissions.

### Systems Introduced
Community inventory, duties, group votes, promises, secrets, rumor/reputation and lightweight leadership.

### NPC Arcs
Maya becomes an increasingly important moral/practical anchor. Marcus demonstrates why authoritarian efficiency can be attractive. Noah can become dependent, useful, resentful or absent based on Act I. Lena can enter through communications expertise. Jonah is heard about before necessarily being met.

### Wake Seeds
- Who receives scarce food/medicine.
- Whether outsiders are admitted.
- Leadership choices.
- Treatment of theft and deception.
- Whether the group shares radio discoveries.
- A promise made during a crisis.

### Returning Wakes
Maya, Noah, the pharmacy encounter and the injured stranger can change who is available, cooperative or hostile.

### Climax
The group receives credible evidence of another organized survivor community.

### Hook
The other community knows something about 6:17 that the player's group does not.

---

# Act III — First Contact
## Days 21–30
### Question: Who can we trust?
### Evolution: NPC World → Human Network

For the first time, another survivor encountered through Fatewake may represent a real player timeline.

### Central Conflict
Contact creates opportunity and vulnerability. Information itself becomes a resource.

### Mystery Progression
- Different groups possess different fragments of the same transmissions.
- Some records disagree on exact wording/timing.
- A location or coordinate repeatedly appears.
- Someone claims to have answered a transmission and received a reply.

### Systems Introduced
Asynchronous player encounters, player-to-player Wakes, offers/counteroffers, limited trade, identity uncertainty and first timeline intersection.

### Multiplayer Rule
The first real-player encounter should be authored as a significant moment. The fiction need not immediately distinguish an NPC from a real player unless that distinction matters.

### Wake Seeds
- First trade.
- First lie to another survivor group.
- Shared intelligence.
- Rescue/refusal.
- Threat or violence.
- First player alliance.

### Returning Wakes
Day 7 signal behavior may affect what information or contact opportunities appear.

### Climax
A decision requires trusting another survivor group with something that cannot easily be recovered: people, supplies, location information or communications access.

### Hook
Contact reveals that isolated communities are beginning to form routes and networks.

---

# Act IV — The Lines We Draw
## Days 31–40
### Question: What belongs to whom?
### Evolution: Group → Community

Survival becomes territorial. Water, medicine, fuel, generators, roads and defensible buildings acquire political meaning.

### Central Conflict
The player must help define the difference between protection, ownership and control.

### Mystery Progression
- The recurring coordinate becomes geographically meaningful.
- Lena finds evidence that at least some transmissions are not behaving like conventional broadcasts.
- A recovered recording contains information no known sender should possess.

### Systems Introduced
Territory, community rules, persistent roles, shared infrastructure, local reputation and conflict escalation.

### Wake Seeds
- Boundary/resource claims.
- Rules for outsiders.
- Punishment and justice.
- Cooperation with neighboring groups.
- Whether force is used preemptively.

### Returning Wakes
First Contact relationships can become trade routes, grudges, warnings or leverage. Early generosity/scarcity choices affect community cohesion.

### Climax
Two groups make incompatible claims over a resource neither can easily survive without.

### Hook
A solution—or failure—pushes multiple communities toward formal settlement organization.

---

# Act V — The Gathering
## Days 41–50
### Question: What are we building?
### Evolution: Communities → Settlements

People stop thinking only about tomorrow. They begin building institutions intended to survive.

### Central Conflict
A settlement needs identity, rules and priorities. Every structure creates winners, obligations and vulnerabilities.

### Mystery Progression
- Survivors correlate 6:17 observations across multiple locations.
- Patterns suggest the phenomenon is larger than any regional disaster.
- Evidence concerning disappearances becomes harder to dismiss.
- The coordinate/pattern may point toward a site, relay or event rather than a simple transmitter.

### Systems Introduced
Settlement founding, population, morale, infrastructure, named roles, persistent policies, larger trade and settlement-level Wakes.

### Wake Seeds
- Settlement charter/leadership.
- Resource priorities.
- Who is permitted to join.
- Treatment of vulnerable/nonproductive survivors.
- Defense philosophy.
- Naming and identity of the settlement.

### Returning Wakes
People helped or harmed in Acts I–III may arrive, send others, refuse assistance or bring skills/resources.

### Climax
The settlement must choose between investing inward or joining a larger network.

### Hook
Organized groups begin offering security, technology, ideology and allegiance.

---

# Act VI — The Fracture
## Days 51–60
### Question: What kind of world replaces the old one?
### Evolution: Settlements → Alliances and Proto-Factions

Survival philosophies harden into political identities. None should be presented as purely correct or evil.

### Central Conflict
Independence has costs. Alliance has costs. Neutrality also becomes a choice with consequences.

### Mystery Progression
- Different organizations offer competing explanations for The Silence.
- Some possess evidence they refuse to share.
- One faction appears unusually prepared for aspects of the catastrophe.
- A 6:17 event reacts differently in different connected communities.

### Systems Introduced
Diplomacy, alliances, proto-factions, treaties, shared defense, intelligence, influence and settlement-to-settlement Wakes.

### Wake Seeds
- Alliance/refusal.
- Intelligence sharing.
- Mutual defense promises.
- Espionage.
- Sanctuary for another group's dissident.
- Choosing ideology versus immediate survival benefit.

### Returning Wakes
Act IV territorial behavior affects reputation. Act V settlement policy determines which alliances are plausible.

### Climax
A crisis forces supposedly allied communities to prove whether their promises mean anything.

### Hook
Old personal choices begin resurfacing at a scale large enough to affect settlements.

---

# Act VII — The Reckoning
## Days 61–70
### Question: What do our old choices cost?
### Evolution: Present Survival → Historical Consequence

This act demonstrates the Wake System's full promise. Events that appeared finished return transformed by time and human memory.

### Central Conflict
Players confront consequences they could not have predicted when making earlier choices.

### Mystery Progression
- Someone can demonstrate knowledge of events from a timeline they seemingly never witnessed.
- Recovered information suggests the 6:17 phenomenon may be connected to the way isolated histories are converging.
- Contradictory memories become evidence rather than noise, without yet explaining why.

### Systems Introduced
Major Wake activation, debts, legacy reputation, historical claims, inherited consequences and broader player-created history.

### Major Wake Returns
Candidate chains include:
- Injured stranger: Day 1 → Act IV hint → Act VII payoff.
- Noah: Day 3 → Act V role → Act VII loyalty/betrayal/rescue.
- Pharmacy group: Day 5 → Act III contact → Act VII alliance or hostility.
- Signal answer: Day 7 → Act III information → Act VII targeted consequence.
- Early resource honesty: Act I/II → settlement culture → Act VII crisis response.

### Climax
A major threat can be solved very differently depending on the relationships and obligations accumulated across the previous sixty days.

### Hook
Multiple timeline networks are being pulled toward the same regional event.

---

# Act VIII — Convergence
## Days 71–80
### Question: Whose history becomes our history?
### Evolution: Separate Timelines → Regional World

Previously independent player histories collide. The game must preserve personal truth while establishing shared regional facts.

### Central Conflict
Communities with incompatible memories, loyalties and histories must cooperate—or decide whose version of events they trust.

### Mystery Progression
- Independent records prove some discrepancies cannot be explained by bad memory alone.
- The 6:17 pattern appears connected to convergence points.
- A forthcoming event can be predicted with increasing confidence.
- Someone is actively trying to influence whether communities connect.

### Systems Introduced
Regional state, convergence, shared historical facts, larger player coalitions, regional votes and scoped Wake propagation.

### Wake Seeds
- Which history/evidence is believed.
- Which communities receive access to the network.
- Whether dangerous information is made regional.
- Whether enemies are allowed into a coalition.
- Who controls communications infrastructure.

### Returning Wakes
Nearly every prior social layer can matter: personal relationships, player encounters, settlement reputation, alliances and betrayals.

### Climax
A regional event forces previously separate timelines into a common authoritative outcome while retaining individual histories.

### Hook
The converged network receives a signal that could not have been understood by any isolated timeline.

---

# Act IX — The Signal
## Days 81–90
### Question: What really happened—and should we answer again?
### Evolution: Regional Survivors → Shared Fatewake World

The first season's systems and story converge. The player learns enough to understand that The Silence is larger and stranger than the original catastrophe model, but not enough to close the mystery.

### Central Conflict
Humanity now has enough connectivity to make a consequential shared decision, but not enough knowledge to know which choice is safe.

### Mystery Progression
- Evidence from different timelines forms a larger pattern.
- Earlier broadcasts gain new meaning when combined.
- At least one widely accepted explanation is proven incomplete or false.
- The source, nature or purpose of The Silence remains unresolved.
- The player learns that what happens next may depend on humanity's collective behavior.

### Systems Introduced
Large-scale shared decisions, mature global/region/player scope, coalition action and the transition toward the persistent Fatewake world.

### Returning Wakes
Act IX should query the entire season rather than one recent decision. The player's allies, enemies, settlement policies, signal history, discoveries, promises and reputation determine their version of the climax.

### Day 90 Climax
A major 6:17 event creates the first truly shared Fatewake-world moment.

The global outcome is aggregated from player behavior, but each player's personal role and consequence remain individualized.

The event must:
- permanently alter world state,
- validate that early decisions mattered,
- create discussion between players,
- reveal meaningful new information,
- preserve the larger mystery,
- create multiple viable futures.

### Final Beat
The player should understand that the first ninety days were not the story.

They were the formation of the world in which the larger story will occur.

> **The first 90 days were the beginning.**

# Cross-Act Wake Lattice

The season should be authored as a lattice rather than an exponentially branching story tree.

| Origin | Intermediate Echo | Major Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 injured stranger | Act IV rumor/contact | Act VII person/network returns |
| Day 1 Maya relationship | Acts II/V leadership tension | Acts VII/IX loyalty consequence |
| Day 3 Noah | Act V settlement role | Act VII loyalty, betrayal or rescue |
| Day 5 pharmacy | Act III survivor contact | Act VII alliance/hostility |
| Day 7 signal | Act III contact differences | Acts VII/IX signal consequence |
| Act II outsider policy | Act V population makeup | Act VIII coalition composition |
| Act III first player contact | Act VI alliance possibility | Act IX shared action |
| Act IV territory dispute | Act VI reputation | Act VIII regional trust |
| Act V settlement identity | Act VI faction options | Act IX political position |
| Act VI promises | Act VII crisis | Act VIII/IX coalition stability |

A Wake does not require one predetermined payoff. It supplies facts, relationships, tags and unresolved obligations that later events can query.

# Multiplayer Escalation

The social scale should expand intentionally:

- **Act I:** NPC-heavy personal survival.
- **Act II:** NPC community and evidence of outsiders.
- **Act III:** first meaningful real-player intersections.
- **Act IV:** recurring player/community relationships.
- **Act V:** settlement-level multiplayer.
- **Act VI:** alliances and proto-factions.
- **Act VII:** player-created history returns.
- **Act VIII:** regional timeline convergence.
- **Act IX:** first large shared-world event.

# Late Joiners

A player joining after the broader Fatewake population has reached later acts still begins at Survivor Day 1.

Their personal timeline progresses through the foundational experience. Convergence mechanisms allow their history to intersect with an existing regional/shared world when narratively and mechanically appropriate.

The exact pacing rules for catching up, timeline speed and convergence eligibility remain to be designed.

# Open Design Questions

The following should be decided before individual Days 8–90 are fully scripted:

1. Exact activity-driven Universe/Timeline Clock rules.
2. How quickly late players can approach convergence.
3. Matchmaking rules for real-player encounters.
4. Offline/proxy behavior for player-controlled actors.
5. Settlement size and governance mechanics.
6. Exact faction identities and when they become canonical.
7. Conflict/PvP safety and consent boundaries.
8. Aggregation rules for regional/global choices.
9. Which Wake data is visible to players.
10. Day 90's exact decision and outcome families.
